Business

The duty

Employment Rights Act 2025 ss.20–22, which amend the Equality Act 2010 ss.40 and 40A. The government timetable (updated 25 September 2026) says these take effect on 30 October 2026. The commencement regulations had not been published on legislation.gov.uk when we checked on 1 October 2026. Applies in England, Wales and Scotland, not Northern Ireland.

Employers must take all reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment of their employees, not merely “reasonable steps”. This is the preventative duty that has applied since 26 October 2024, now with a higher bar. From the same date, an employer must not permit a third party (a customer, client, contractor or member of the public) to harass an employee in the course of their employment. An employer “permits” it if it failed to take all reasonable steps to prevent it. The third-party rule covers harassment related to age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex and sexual orientation, not only sexual harassment.

If a sexual harassment claim succeeds and the tribunal finds a breach of the preventative duty, it may increase the compensation by up to 25% (Equality Act 2010 s.124A). This is not a fine, and staff cannot bring a claim for breach of the duty on its own. The EHRC can take enforcement action even when nobody has brought a claim. The new power to set out specific “reasonable steps” in regulations (s.22) does not replace the general duty. GOV.UK expects those regulations in 2027/28 at the earliest, after consultation. The higher standard applies first. Since 6 April 2026, disclosures about sexual harassment can be protected whistleblowing disclosures (s.23).

Not legal advice. All Steps helps you organise and record what you have done. It cannot tell you that you have taken all reasonable steps: that depends on your workplace, and a tribunal decides it case by case. This is not an official or EHRC-approved checklist, and there is no statutory Code of Practice on harassment. Use the EHRC technical guidance on sexual harassment and harassment at work (the EHRC says it is updating it for the 2026 changes), and take advice from a qualified adviser on a live complaint.
